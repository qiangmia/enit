Stolen Passwords, Stolen Data

Adapted from BBC News, "North Face and Cartier customer data stolen in cyber attacks" (3 June 2025)
🗞️ BBC Technology ⏱️ Reading time: 6-8 minutes

Fashion brand The North Face and luxury jeweller Cartier have become the latest retailers to report that customer data has been stolen in cyber attacks.

North Face emailed some customers to say it had discovered a "small-scale" attack in April. Cartier said that "an unauthorised party gained temporary access to our system". Both brands said that data such as customers' names and email addresses was taken, but that financial information was not.

This is part of a wave of attacks on well-known retailers. Adidas, Victoria's Secret and Harrods have all reported incidents in recent weeks. Marks and Spencer and the Co-op had their operations severely disrupted when they were targeted in April.

The most important detail is how the North Face attack happened. The company told customers that the hackers used a technique called credential stuffing. In this type of attack, criminals take usernames and passwords that were stolen in an earlier breach and try them on a different website. They are betting that customers have reused the same password on more than one account.

If the bet works, the criminals get in. North Face said the attackers may have been able to see some users' shipping addresses and purchase histories. Affected customers were told they would need to change their passwords. Cartier, by contrast, said that passwords and card details had not been accessed, and that it had "contained the issue".

Why are shops such a popular target? "Retailers are overflowing with customer information," said James Hadley, founder of the cyber-security company Immersive. That makes them "easy targets for attackers". He also warned that stolen data can be used much later, to impersonate a real company and trick people into giving away even more. Criminals are, he said, "often content to play the long game".

credentials
登入憑證
A username and password used to sign in
🧪
credential stuffing
憑證填充攻擊
Trying stolen logins on other websites
💥
breach
資料外洩事件
An incident where data is accessed without permission
💳
financial information
財務資訊
Card numbers, bank details and payment data
🎭
impersonate

To pretend to be someone or a company
🛑
contain
控制住
To stop a problem from spreading further
APart 1: Multiple Choice (4 questions)

1. What is credential stuffing?

2. Credential stuffing works because many people ______.

3. According to the article, what was taken from North Face customers?

4. Why did James Hadley say retailers are "easy targets"?

BPart 2: True / False / Not Given (2 questions)

Write TRUE, FALSE, or NOT GIVEN.

  1. Cartier said that customers' card details were not accessed. __________
  2. North Face has said exactly how many customers were affected. __________
CPart 3: Short Answer (2 questions)

Answer in complete sentences, using your own words where possible.

  1. Why is stolen customer data still dangerous months after the attack?
  2. What were North Face customers told to do after the attack?
🔑Answers

Part 1: Multiple Choice

  1. B – trying stolen logins on other websites.
  2. B – they reuse the same password on several accounts.
  3. B – names and email addresses.
  4. B – they store a lot of customer information.

Part 2: True / False / Not Given

  1. TRUE – "Passwords and card details were not accessed, it said."
  2. NOT GIVEN – the article says the attack was "small-scale" but gives no number.

Part 3: Short Answer

  1. It can be used later to impersonate a real company and trick people into giving away more information — criminals "play the long game".
  2. They were told to change their passwords.
🛡️Spot the Phish

Task (10 minutes): In pairs, read the two messages below. Decide which one is suspicious and give at least two reasons. Then write one sentence of advice.

Message A
From: The North Face <account@northface-support.info>
Subject: "URGENT: your account data was stolen"
Body: "To protect your account, confirm your password and card number in the next 2 hours or your account will be closed."
Message B
From: The North Face <no-reply@thenorthface.com>
Subject: "Security notice — please reset your password"
Body: "We have contained a security incident. As a precaution, please change your password by signing in to our website directly. We will never ask for your password by email."

Hints: check the sender address, the urgency, and whether the message asks you to send a password or card number.
