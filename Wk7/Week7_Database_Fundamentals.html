“What Did You Do Yesterday?” — Past Simple Chain

Setup

Nothing to prepare. This is a speaking warm-up in pairs.

Activity

Take turns describing what you did yesterday morning, one action at a time. Your partner must ask a follow-up question about each action. Keep it to the past.

Example

A: “I went to the library at nine.” B: “Did you study there?” A: “I did. I read for two hours, then I came home.”

Full Text: "What Happened to the Records"

🔊 Listen to this article
What Happened to the Records
A backup failed at a hospital, and nobody noticed for six hours
Name: Huang Chia-Yu (黃范尚) Age: 31 Job: Database Administrator
Company: A regional hospital, Tainan Education: M.S. in Information Science

Yesterday 14:20 — The Alert

Chia-Yu is a database administrator at a hospital. Yesterday at 14:20 her phone rang. A backup job had failed. Nobody noticed for six hours. At first she did not understand the message, because the system reported “0 files copied” instead of “job failed”, and she had seen that message before without worrying about it.

Yesterday 14:35 — The Cause

She logged in and looked at the disk. The server had run out of space at 13:04. Nobody had deleted a file. A log file had simply grown until it filled the disk. She found the folder, checked the file sizes, and found one file of 41 gigabytes. The system had been writing the same message since 2019.

Yesterday 15:10 — The Fix

Chia-Yu moved the file, freed the space, and restarted the job. It finished at 16:30. Then she checked the records, because a failed backup is only a problem if the live data is also damaged. Nothing was lost. She wrote down the time, the cause, and the fix.

Yesterday 17:45 — The Report

At 17:45 she wrote the incident report. It was only one page. “The backup failed at 13:04 because the disk was full. The reason was a log file of 41 gigabytes. I moved the file at 15:10 and the job finished at 16:30. In future, the server will send an email when the disk is above 80%.”

This Morning 09:00 — The Change

This morning she added the alert and changed one setting: log files are now deleted after thirty days. Her manager read the report and asked only one question. “Why did nobody notice for six hours?” Chia-Yu did not have a good answer, and she wrote that sentence into the second page of the report.

"A report is mostly past tense, but the last sentence is future. “It failed” tells us what happened. “It will send an email” tells us what changes." — — Huang Chia-Yu, Database Administrator

Quick Review: Past Simple Forms

The past simple describes actions that are finished. The form of the verb changes, but the time of speaking does not change.

PatternExample
regular verb + -edThe backup failed at 13:04.
irregular verbThe disk ran out of space.
was / wereThe server was full.
did not + base verbShe didn't delete anything.
Did + subject + base verb?Did anyone notice the failure?
Key Rules
  • Use the past simple with a finished time: yesterday, last week, in 2019, at 14:20, three days ago.
  • Regular verbs take -ed. After -e add only -d (move → moved). Consonant + y becomes -ied (apply → applied). Double the consonant after a short vowel (stop → stopped).
  • Irregular verbs must be memorised: was/were, ran, took, found, wrote, lost, gave, kept, broke, sent.
  • Negative: did not + base verb — never 'did not deleted'.
  • Question: Did + subject + base verb? — never 'Did she deleted'.
  • If the time is not finished, use the present perfect: The job failed yesterday (finished) vs The job has failed twice this month (unfinished).
Common Mistakes (Chinese L1 Speakers)

Chinese speakers often add -ed after did.
✗ She did not deleted the file. → ✓ She did not delete the file.
✗ Did she wrote the report? → ✓ Did she write the report?

Also avoid the past simple with an unfinished time:
✗ I have finished my homework last night. → ✓ I finished my homework last night.

Activity A: Past Simple or Present Perfect?

Decide which tense each sentence needs, and say why.

  1. 1. The disk ___ (fill) up at 13:04 yesterday. filled — finished time
  2. 2. The server has ___ (run) out of space three times this month. run — unfinished time
  3. 3. Nobody ___ (notice) the failure for six hours. noticed — past simple (no did)
  4. 4. ___ she move the file? Did ... move — no -ed after did
  5. 5. We ___ (not delete) anything, so nothing was lost. didn't delete — base verb after didn't
Example

“Sentence 2 is the trap: three times this month is not finished, so it needs the present perfect. Yesterday needs the past simple.”

Activity B: Write an Incident Report

Invent a small technical problem from last week and write a report in five sentences. Then read it to a partner, who must ask three follow-up questions in the past simple.

Try: “The classroom projector failed at 10:00. The technician came at 10:40. He replaced the cable, but the screen stayed white. He found a loose connection, fixed it, and the projector worked by 11:15. Nobody noticed the problem for twenty minutes.”
