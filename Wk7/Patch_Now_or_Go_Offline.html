🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

  1. Read an authentic BBC News report and identify its main claim, key facts, and the people and organisations involved.
  2. Explain what PHP is, what a vulnerability is, and why a software patch can be urgent.
  3. Use the past simple to report finished events, and recognise why news writing also uses the present perfect and passive forms.
  4. Distinguish fact from attribution, and notice how reporting verbs, modals and quotations show how certain a claim is.
  5. Deliver a 60-second incident briefing with a clear recommendation.

Cyber chiefs tell websites to make critical safety update or go offline
The complete BBC News report by Tom Gerken, Technology reporter (21 October 2026)
Source: BBC News Text: Full, unabridged Level: B2 (Upper-intermediate) Time: ~10 minutes

Organisations which rely on the PHP programming language are being told they should install a significant software update today - or consider taking their websites offline.

Two critical vulnerabilities with PHP, which is widely used across the internet, have been identified.

The PHP Foundation, which supports it, is issuing updates which address them on Wednesday afternoon.

The UK’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), as well as its counterparts in the EU and Australia have urged those who use PHP to apply the fixes as soon as they become available.

The Netherlands’ cyber-security agency says it is taking its own website offline due to the vulnerabilities identified.

The country’s National Institute for Public Health and the Environment, an agency of the Dutch government, has taken down its website.

Similarly the website of the Tweede Kamer, the lower house of the nation’s parliament, has become inaccessible.

“Where immediate remediation is not feasible, organisations should consider taking the system offline until it can be fully mitigated,” the NCSC said in a note.

Japan’s national cyber-security office and EU cyber-security officials have issued a similar warning.

“PHP is everywhere. It quietly powers WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, and Magento sites, shared hosting platforms, control panels, and an awful lot of software that doesn’t even advertise that it runs PHP under the hood,” cyber-security company Huntress said.

Cyber-security expert Don Smith, from the Sophos Counter Threat Unit, told the BBC this would “likely be a priority target” for hackers.

The immediate challenge was not simply installing a patch to fix the issue, but finding and managing every instance where PHP was running, he said.

“The use of PHP is pervasive, it can sit beneath public websites, content management systems, hosting platforms and third-party products, sometimes without even being obvious to the organisation using it.”

He said it was crucial that organisations looked out for signs that an attacker “might have already have gained a foothold”.

‘Early attacks emerging’

The European Union Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-EU) said the risk scores given to the vulnerabilities “indicate the potential for unauthenticated remote code execution”.

Such a cyber-attack could potentially allow hackers to install malware and take down websites.

Dane Knecht, chief technology officer at internet infrastructure firm Cloudflare, said he was “seeing early attacks emerging and are updating mitigations as new vectors show up”.

The NCSC said once organisations have installed the update, they should “continuously monitor for security updates from third-party software vendors, including for appliances and services that may bundle PHP”.

It is not yet clear which versions of the programming language are affected, but CERT-EU said it could affect all versions of it dating back to PHP 5 onwards - which launched in 2004.

PHP was created for web development. It is used on servers to implement what you see when you visit websites.

Not all websites use it, but it nonetheless powers a large portion of the internet.

👇 Headline Analysis

Look at the headline again: “Cyber chiefs tell websites to make critical safety update or go offline.”

  1. Who are the “cyber chiefs”? Name at least two from the article.
  2. What is the “critical safety update”?
  3. Why does the headline use “or”? What are the two choices?
  4. The body uses “cyber-security” but the headline uses “safety.” Why might an editor choose “safety” for a general audience?
🔑 Show discussion points

1. The UK’s NCSC, its EU and Australian counterparts, Japan’s national cyber-security office, and CERT-EU.

2. The update from the PHP Foundation that addresses two critical vulnerabilities in PHP.

3. It presents two options: install the update, or take the website offline until it can be fixed.

4. “Safety” is broader and more familiar than “security”; it makes the warning sound relevant to ordinary readers, not only technical specialists.

D. Who says it? Attribution and hedging

News writers do not present every sentence as equally certain. Complete the table, then check your answers.

On a phone, swipe the table sideways to see all three columns.

Sentence from the reportWho is responsible?How certain?
“Two critical vulnerabilities ... have been identified.”
“This would ‘likely be a priority target’ for hackers.”
“Such a cyber-attack could potentially allow hackers to install malware...”
“Seeing early attacks emerging...”
“It is not yet clear which versions ... are affected.”
🔑 Show answers

1. The BBC report — presented as fact.

2. Don Smith, Sophos — a prediction, softened by “likely.”

3. The report, based on CERT-EU — a possibility, softened by “could potentially.”

4. Dane Knecht, Cloudflare — a company observation.

5. The BBC report — uncertainty, marked by “not yet clear.”

Why it matters: reporting verbs (said, told, urged) and modals (could, may, would) show how certain a claim is. This is called hedging.

⚠️ Six Phrases That Will Block You

These are the hardest parts of the report. Tap each one to open it.

MeaningA weakness that is serious enough to need immediate action.

In contextThe report says two critical vulnerabilities in PHP have been identified.

Try it: “The team fixed a critical vulnerability before attackers could use it.”

MeaningTo install the repaired version of the software.

In contextAgencies urged PHP users to apply the fixes as soon as they became available.

Try it: “We applied the fix as soon as the vendor released it.”

MeaningTo disconnect a system from the internet so that attackers cannot reach it.

In contextThe NCSC said organisations should consider taking the system offline if they could not fix it immediately.

Try it: “We took the server offline until the patch was ready.”

MeaningAn attacker can make a server run their own code from another location, often without logging in.

In contextCERT-EU warned about the potential for unauthenticated remote code execution.

Try it: “Remote code execution is one of the most serious kinds of vulnerability.”

MeaningInside the system; not visible to the user.

In contextHuntress says a lot of software runs PHP under the hood without advertising it.

Try it: “The app looks simple, but there is a lot happening under the hood.”

MeaningTo get a first small position inside a system, from which an attacker can do more.

In contextDon Smith said organisations should look for signs that an attacker might have already gained a foothold.

Try it: “The attacker gained a foothold through an old plugin.”

💬 Discussion

  1. The report says “PHP is everywhere” but also that “not all websites use it.” Why can invisible software be a bigger risk than software you can see?
  2. Who should be responsible for patching: the PHP Foundation (which writes the update), the organisations that use PHP, or the hosting companies? Explain your answer.
  3. “Take the website offline” protects users but costs money. When is that the right decision?
  4. If you were the IT manager of a small company, what would you do in the first hour after this warning? Make a three-step list.
  5. Do news reports like this help the public, or do they mainly spread fear? Explain.

🔧 Activity: The 60-Second Incident Briefing (15 minutes)

Work in groups of three or four. You are the IT team for a university club website that runs on WordPress. The warning has just arrived. Prepare a 60-second briefing for the club president.

Use this structure:

  1. What happened: two critical PHP vulnerabilities; the PHP Foundation issued updates on Wednesday; agencies urged immediate action.
  2. Why it matters to us: our WordPress site uses PHP, and we may not know every plugin or service that uses it.
  3. What we need: permission to patch tonight, audit our plugins, and prepare a backup plan.
  4. What we recommend: patch now, monitor for attacks, and take the site offline if we cannot patch immediately.

Language checklist: use at least three past simple verbs, one present perfect sentence, one passive sentence, and four target vocabulary items.

Peer feedback. Listen to another group. Did they state the facts accurately? Did they explain why the club is affected? Did they make one clear recommendation? Did they use the target grammar and vocabulary?

🏠 Wrap-up

  • PHP is a server-side programming language that powers a large part of the web, often invisibly.
  • Two critical vulnerabilities led to an emergency update and warnings from multiple agencies.
  • Organisations had two choices: patch immediately or take systems offline.
  • Patching is also an inventory problem: finding every instance is the hard part.
  • Past simple reports finished events; present perfect links news to the present; passive keeps the focus on the event.
