By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
  1. Identify at least 10 common computer hardware components and their functions.

  3. Read and comprehend a short article about building a computer.
  4. Expand hardware vocabulary including internal components, peripherals, and technical specifications.

Full Text: "Building My First Computer"

🔊 Listen to this article
Building My First Computer
A Taiwanese student's hands-on learning experience
Name: Chen Meilin (陳美玲) Age: 20 Job: Information Management Student
Company: Yuan Ze University Education: Second-year student

Saturday Morning — The Decision

Meilin decides to build her own desktop computer. She wants to understand how hardware works. Her friend Wei, a computer engineering student, offers to help. They go to a computer market in Taipei to buy the parts.

At the Computer Market — Choosing the Parts

There are many stores in the market. Each store specializes in different components. Meilin needs a processor, a motherboard, memory, storage, a power supply, a case, and a cooling system. Wei explains that the processor is the most important part. There are two main brands: Intel and AMD. Meilin chooses an Intel Core i5 processor.

Afternoon — Building the Computer

Back in Meilin's dorm room, she opens the case. There is a lot of space inside. First, she installs the motherboard. Then she puts the processor into the motherboard. There are small pins on the motherboard — she must be careful. Next, she installs the memory sticks. There are two memory slots, and she fills both with 8GB sticks.

Evening — First Boot

Meilin connects the power supply and turns on the computer. There is a moment of silence. Then the fan starts spinning. The screen shows the BIOS. Everything works. Wei says, 'There is no feeling like turning on something you built yourself.' Meilin agrees.

"Building a computer taught me more about hardware than any textbook. When you hold the parts in your hands, you understand how they work together." — — Chen Meilin, Information Management Student

Review: Singular vs. Plural

Use there is with one item (singular) and there are with multiple items (plural).

PatternExample
There is + singular nounThere is a CPU on the motherboard.
There are + plural nounThere are two memory slots.
There isn't + singularThere isn't a graphics card in this computer.
There aren't + pluralThere aren't enough USB ports.
Key Rules
  • There is = There's (contraction): There's a problem with the hard drive.
  • There are = There're (less common in speech): There are three fans in the case.
  • Negative: There isn't / There aren't: There isn't enough memory. There aren't any expansion slots.
  • Questions: Is there...? / Are there...?: Is there a warranty? Are there any free ports?
Common Mistakes (Chinese L1 Speakers)

Chinese speakers: Don't use "have" instead of "there is/are." ✗ My computer has two USB ports on the side. → ✓ There are two USB ports on the side of my computer.

Activity B: Describe Your Computer
