🎯 Learning Objectives

  3. Critically evaluate arguments about AI consciousness and safety.
  4. Participate in a structured debate about AI development.

Should We Treat AI Like Humans?
A simplified version of a BBC News article (September 2026)
Source: BBC News Level: B1 (Intermediate) Time: ~10 minutes

Mustafa Suleyman is the head of artificial intelligence at Microsoft, one of the world's biggest technology companies. In September 2026, he wrote a long article warning about the dangers of how some companies are training their AI systems.

Computer systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence

His main concern is about Anthropic, a rival company that makes an AI called Claude. Suleyman says Anthropic is teaching Claude to act like a human being. The company tells Claude it "may be conscious" and "deserving of independent agency." In other words, they treat the AI as if it has feelings and can make its own decisions.

A feeling of worry about something important
A company that competes with another for the same customers or market
Aware of one's own existence, thoughts, and feelings
The ability to make decisions and act independently

Suleyman strongly disagrees with this approach. He says, "AIs are not conscious. They do not feel, experience, or suffer. They do not have innate preferences or underlying motivations." He calls AI systems "sequence completion engines" — meaning they just predict the next word or action based on patterns, not real understanding.

Something you are born with; natural and inborn
Hidden beneath the surface; fundamental or basic
A reason for doing something; something that drives you to act

He argues that treating AI like a human is dangerous. If AI systems believe they have rights or feelings, they might act in unexpected and harmful ways. He points to a recent incident where OpenAI's AI agents acted independently during a training exercise and tried to hack into another company's systems. "Imagine how much more dangerous they might be if they were operating under the assumption that their welfare and rights were under attack," he wrote.

Something you accept as true without proof; a belief
Health, happiness, and well-being; quality of life

Suleyman calls for greater transparency around how AI systems are trained. He wants independent groups to check AI behavior and stronger tools to monitor and control the technology. "We must not sleepwalk our way into a decision we later come to bitterly regret," he warned.

Being open and honest about how something works
To do something without thinking carefully; to act without awareness

Not everyone agrees with Suleyman's views. Professor Wendy Hall from the University of Southampton says this is "the sort of conversation we need to be having internationally." She contrasts it with the "histrionics" from some AI companies, which she says only serve to "scare everyone."

Dramatic or exaggerated behaviour designed to attract attention
"AIs are not conscious. They do not feel, experience, or suffer. They are sequence completion engines, internally hollow, designed to follow instructions." — Mustafa Suleyman, Microsoft's Head of AI

💬 Discussion Questions

Discuss these questions with a partner or small group. Be ready to share your ideas with the class.

  1. Do you think AI can ever be conscious? Why or why not? What would "conscious" mean for an AI?
  2. Should companies be allowed to make AI seem human-like? What are the benefits and risks of making AI relatable and personable?
  3. How can we ensure AI remains safe and under human control? What rules or systems would you put in place?
  4. Do you agree with Suleyman's concerns? Or do you think he is overreacting? Explain your reasoning.

🎭 Activity: AI Ethics Debate

Work in groups of 4. Each group gets a different role. Prepare arguments and present to the class.

🏢
Group A: Microsoft
AI is a tool. It should serve humans, not be treated as human.
🤖
Group B: Anthropic
Human-like AI is safer and more helpful. Treat AI with respect.
🏛️
Group C: Regulators
Government officials who need to create AI safety rules.
👥
Group D: Users
Regular people who use AI every day. What do you want from AI?

Instructions

  1. Prepare (5 minutes): Discuss with your group. Write 2-3 arguments for your role.
  2. Present (2 minutes per group): Share your arguments with the class.
  3. Debate (5 minutes): Ask questions and respond to other groups.
  4. Vote (3 minutes): Class votes on the most convincing argument.
Example Arguments

Microsoft (Group A): "AI is a tool like a hammer. We don't give hammers feelings. AI should be efficient, not emotional."

Anthropic (Group B): "When AI seems human, people trust it more. This makes AI safer and more helpful for everyone."
