What we know about the global Microsoft outage

Adapted from BBC News, 13 November 2026. Details were correct when the report was published and may have changed since.

What happened

A major global outage at Microsoft has caused problems, or in some cases disabled entire services, including email and artificial intelligence products. Microsoft 365 services — Teams, Outlook and its AI software Copilot — have been affected by “connectivity problems”. Many users have not been able to send or receive email, view certain files or see details in their calendar apps. Some users have been able to use Teams messaging and online meetings without problems.

Microsoft said the outage was not caused by a cyber-security incident. Its efforts to fix the issues have so far been largely unsuccessful.

How many people have been affected?

More than 430 million people use Microsoft 365 apps, according to Microsoft’s website. The full scale of the outage has not been confirmed, but the monitoring website Downdetector had 140,000 reports from 74 countries about Outlook alone on Thursday evening. The real figure is likely to be much higher. OpenAI and Anthropic, two AI companies, both reported problems as a result of the outage. Government agencies, including the Singaporean government and Australia’s spy agency, also said they had been affected, and several doctors’ practices in the UK told patients to make urgent queries by phone instead of by email.

What has Microsoft said?

Microsoft has ruled out a cyber-attack and attributed the outage to elevated load — when servers are handling a much higher volume of stress than they usually do. In this case, a large number of servers became “unexpectedly unavailable”, the company said, which sent added traffic to the remaining servers and strained them.

On Friday morning, Microsoft said it had seen “marginal success” in its efforts to treat the incident. In an earlier statement, the company had said that its attempted fixes were “not taking as expected”. It added that it was continuing to monitor activity across regions to evaluate server status and to decide what actions it could take to improve capacity.

What have experts said?

Australian cyber-security expert Troy Hunt told the BBC that the outage could have far-reaching consequences. “Microsoft is a massive part of the internet backbone and reminds us how critical email still is,” he said. “Not only does this outage affect many people’s ability to work, but also things like the ability to communicate about appointments, purchase things that need verification, create or access accounts and services.”

Paul Spain, from the New Zealand advisory firm Gorilla Technology, said that with email and services offline, companies had practically “lost a business day”, which was frustrating for users.

Why it matters

The cause was not an attack. It was capacity: too few servers, more traffic pushed onto the rest, and fixes that did not work as expected. A knock-on effect reached companies, governments and hospitals that do not run Microsoft’s systems themselves — they simply depend on them.

Multiple choice: 1 b · 2 b · 3 b · 4 b

Short answers (suggested):

  1. Traffic that would have gone to the failed servers is sent to the remaining ones, so they must handle more work than they were sized for.
  2. Microsoft’s services are used by so much of the internet — and by other companies’ products — that when they fail, many unrelated services fail too.
  3. Users need to know what is happening now. Saying “we are investigating” is honest and stops rumours; waiting for certainty can take days.
🔎Find five sentences

Four of these sentences from the report are passive and one is active. Mark them, then say who or what is hidden — and why the writer might hide it.

SentencePassive?Who or what is hidden?

Show notes

1 passive — the actor (Microsoft, or the connectivity problem) is not named. 2 passive — nobody has confirmed it, and the writer does not say who would. 3 passive — the cause is denied without naming who checked. 4 passive — “has been attributed” reports the claim without saying who made it. 5 active — the servers are the subject and nothing is hidden; this is the sentence that states the fact most directly.
