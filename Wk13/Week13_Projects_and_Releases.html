This week: Three days before a client demo, a stakeholder asks for “one small feature”. You will read how a four-person team decided what to cut, and practise the verb patterns English uses for deciding, avoiding and suggesting.

Before you read: Think of a group project you have worked on. Did it grow while you were working on it? Who decided what to leave out?

🎯By the end of this week, you will be able to
  • read a project timeline and say what happened in what order;
  • explain scope creep and why it is expensive;
  • use gerunds and infinitives after common verbs;
  • write a three-line status update for a stakeholder;
  • explain what a retrospective is for.

Read: The Sprint That Grew

Read along: tap ▶ for narration. Tap a highlighted word for its definition.

Chen Po-Han is 25 and works as a junior developer at a small app studio in Taipei. His team has four people and one sprint of two weeks. On Monday, three days before a client demo, the backlog was empty enough to feel calm. Then a message arrived.

The client’s marketing manager wanted “one small export feature” before the demo. Po-Han estimated it at two days. His stakeholder said it would take one. The team agreed to try.

Small requests are still requests.

The team’s retrospective, written on the board

By Tuesday afternoon the feature had grown: first a date filter, then a choice of file format, then a logo on every page. This is scope creep: a project that gets larger one reasonable request at a time.

On Wednesday morning the team met for twenty minutes. Their milestone — the demo — could not move. The new feature was now a blocker for two other deliverables. “We have two options,” the team lead said. “We can delay the export, or we can ship it without the filters.”

They decided to cut the logo, postpone the filters, and keep the basic export. Po-Han avoided estimating in front of the client again; he wrote the estimate down instead. The team prioritised the demo, signed off the shorter scope in writing, and shipped on Thursday night.

On Friday the demo went well. The client asked when the filters would arrive, and the answer was a date, not a promise.

In the retrospective that afternoon, the team wrote one sentence on the board: “Small requests are still requests.” The handover note for the next sprint listed the postponed work, with the estimate that had been ignored on Monday.

Original story for this course.

📚Vocabulary: running a project

📐Grammar: Gerunds and Infinitives (verb patterns)

Some verbs are followed by to + verb; others by -ing. The pattern belongs to the verb, not to the meaning, so it has to be learned with the verb.

FormExample
decide / agree / plan / hope + to + verbThe team decided to cut the export.
avoid / finish / keep / suggest + -ingThey avoided delaying the demo.
preposition + -ingThey shipped without testing the export.
worth + -ingIt is worth testing the login flow first.
stop + -ing / stop + to + verbThey stopped adding features. / They stopped to check the backlog.

Key rules

  • Verbs of planning and deciding usually take to + verb: decide, agree, plan, hope, promise, manage, fail.
  • Verbs of avoiding and continuing usually take -ing: avoid, finish, keep, enjoy, suggest, mind, consider.
  • After a preposition, always -ing: before shipping, after finishing, without testing.
  • Suggest never takes to + verb: suggest moving, not suggest to move.
  • Stop + -ing ends an activity; stop + to + verb pauses in order to do something.

Common mistakes

Two errors appear again and again: ✗ They decided cutting the feature. → ✓ They decided to cut the feature. and ✗ She suggested to move the release. → ✓ She suggested moving the release.

Also watch the preposition: ✗ without to test → ✓ without testing.

Activity A: Choose the pattern

Complete each sentence with the correct form of the verb.

  1. The team decided ___ (cut) the export feature.
  2. They avoided ___ (delay) the demo.
  3. It is worth ___ (test) the login flow first.
  4. The client suggested ___ (move) the release.
  5. We managed ___ (ship) on Friday.
Show answers
  1. to cut
  2. delaying
  3. testing
  4. moving
  5. to ship

Activity B: Write the status update

Write three sentences to a stakeholder about a project that is behind. Use one to + verb pattern, one -ing pattern, and one preposition + -ing.

✓ We decided to postpone the filter until the next sprint. We have avoided changing the demo date. Before shipping, we will test the export with real data.
🔁Rephrasing

Verb patterns are a rephrasing tool: the same decision can be written as a clause or as a gerund/infinitive phrase.

Clause → infinitive
Original: They decided that they would cut the feature.
Rephrased: They decided to cut the feature.
Two sentences → one
Original: The team finished the migration. Then they tested it.
Rephrased: After finishing the migration, the team tested it.

Try it

  1. We agreed that we would move the release.
  2. They kept adding features, and the scope grew.
  3. Before we signed off, we tested the export.
Show model answers
  1. We agreed to move the release.
  2. They kept adding features, which caused scope creep.
  3. Before signing off, we tested the export.
