Full Text: "The Handover Note"

🔊 Listen to this article
The Handover Note
What a departing developer leaves behind
Name: Lin Min-Ju (林民建) Age: 24 Job: Junior Web Developer
Company: An online clothing retailer, Taipei Education: B.S. in Information Management

Monday 10:00 AM — The Handover

Min-Ju starts her first week as a web developer at an online shop. On Monday morning her manager gives her a folder of documents left behind by a colleague who is leaving the company. At the top of the notes is a single line: “You must read all of this before you change anything.”

Tuesday 9:30 AM — The Checklist

Tuesday morning Min-Ju opens the checklist. It is longer than she expected. “The design team must approve every new page. You have to run the tests before each release, and you should ask your manager before anything goes live. And you mustn't edit the live site directly — you are supposed to work on the test version first. One more thing: you don't have to update the content every day. That only matters during the sale weeks.” Min-Ju writes both of the last two sentences on a sticky note, because they look almost identical and they are not.

Wednesday 3:00 PM — The Question

On Wednesday a designer asks Min-Ju to add a sale banner today. She wants to say yes, but the checklist says approvals must come first. So she asks her manager. “Must I get approval for a banner, or should I just add it?” Her manager smiles. “Good question. You must get approval — but you don't have to get it twice.”

Thursday 11:00 PM — Launch

On Thursday evening the new site goes live and the whole team watches the visitor numbers. Nothing breaks. Min-Ju reads the checklist one more time, and notices something that is not in it at all: what she has to learn is how to say no politely to people who outrank her.

"The most useful line in the whole folder was the shortest one: you must read all of this before you change anything." — — Lin Min-Ju, Junior Web Developer

Quick Review: Four Ways to Say “You Ought To”

These modals all talk about obligation, but they differ in how strong the obligation is and where it comes from.

PatternExample
must + base verbYou must read the documentation first.
have to + base verbYou have to run the tests before the release.
should + base verbYou should ask your manager first.
be supposed to + base verbYou are supposed to work on the test version.
Key Rules
  • must and have to are nearly the same. must often comes from a written rule; have to often comes from the situation.
  • mustn't = it is forbidden. You mustn't edit the live site.
  • don't have to = it is not necessary. You don't have to update the content daily.
  • should = advice, not a rule. You should check with your manager.
  • be supposed to = an expectation, softer than must. You're supposed to file a ticket first.
  • Questions: Must I…? Do I have to…? Should I…? Am I supposed to…?
Common Mistakes (Chinese L1 Speakers)

The classic error: mustn't does NOT mean ‘don't have to’.
✗ You mustn't update the content every day. (= it is forbidden)
→ ✓ You don't have to update the content every day. (= it is not necessary)

Also, there is no to after a modal: ✗ I must to go. → ✓ I must go.

Activity A: Which Modal?

Choose the modal that matches the meaning. Then explain your choice to a partner.

  1. 1. You ___ touch the server. It will stop. mustn't — forbidden
  2. 2. You ___ wear a helmet. The company requires it. must / have to — external rule
  3. 3. You ___ buy a licence for this software, but you ___ buy one if you use it commercially. don't have to ... must — not necessary vs necessary
  4. 4. You ___ book the meeting room earlier next time. should — advice
  5. 5. Students ___ use their own laptops during the exam. are supposed to — expectation
Example

“Sentence 3 is the important one: don't have to and must are opposites, and mixing them up reverses the meaning.”

Activity B: Write Your Own Checklist

Write a six-rule checklist for a new intern on a project you know. Use a different modal in each rule, and underline it.

Try: “You must wear a helmet. You don't have to bring your own laptop. You should ask before changing the schedule. You mustn't share the password. You're supposed to write a handover note. You have to submit timesheets by Friday.”
