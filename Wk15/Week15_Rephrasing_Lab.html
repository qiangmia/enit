🔁The Five Moves

A rephrase is not a synonym hunt. It is a change of structure that keeps the information. These are the five moves, with a sentence from Week 11.

1. Change the subject
Original: “The team rolled back the change.”
Rephrased: “The change was rolled back.”
2. Change the voice
Original: “The replica was saturated.”
Rephrased: “The replica could not handle the traffic.”
3. Split a long sentence
Original: “The rule, which had been tested in staging, failed when a second region was added.”
Rephrased: “The rule had been tested in staging. It failed when a second region was used.”
4. Join two short ones
Original: “The demo was close. The team cut the export.”
Rephrased: “Because the demo was close, the team cut the export.”
5. Swap the vocabulary
Original: “The replica was saturated.”
Rephrased: “The replica was full.”
Careful
“No data was lost” ≠ “Some data was lost.” A rephrase keeps who, what, when, how much — and keeps not.

  2. If companies automate routine tasks, junior roles will change.
  3. The government said that the ban would come into force in 2027.
  4. YouTube said it was a vital resource for young people.

Show model answers
  1. The team rolled back the change at 03:11. (change the subject)
  2. Automation of routine tasks will change junior roles. (nominalise)
  3. According to the government, the ban will start in 2027. (quote → source)
  4. YouTube described itself as important for young people. (swap the vocabulary)
  5. Because the demo was close, the team cut the export feature. (join)
  6. In the most exposed sectors, employment fell by 12.8%, a study found. (move the source)
  7. The rule had been tested in staging, but it failed when a second region was added. (split)
  8. The replica was still running, but it could not handle the traffic. (swap the vocabulary)

📐Grammar: Mixed Review

Five minutes, ten sentences. Decide which of the four structures each sentence needs, then write it.

FormExample
PassiveA change was made by someone.
ConditionalA real or imagined future, with a result.
Gerund / infinitiveA decision, an avoidance, or a suggestion.
Reported speechWhat someone said, without quotation marks.

Key rules

  • If the actor is unknown or unimportant, the passive is shorter and calmer.
  • If the sentence has if, check the verb in both halves.
  • If two verbs meet, the first one decides whether the second takes -ing or to.
  • If you report a claim, move the tense back and keep the reporting verb honest.
  • Never change not, a number, a date or a name. Those are the facts.

Common mistakes

The most expensive rephrasing error is dropping a negative: “Not all economists agree” becomes “economists agree”. The second most expensive is changing a number because the sentence read better.
