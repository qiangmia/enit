🔊 Listen to this article
The Week That Went in Three Tenses
A junior developer's sprint, in three different kinds of future
Name: Chen Wei-Hao (陳御豚) Age: 23 Job: Junior Software Developer
Company: A mobile app company, Taichung Education: B.S. in Information Management

Monday 9:15 AM — The Plan

Wei-Hao has been a developer for four months. This morning his team is planning the next two-week sprint. “We are going to add a search function,” his manager says. “The customers have been asking for it since July. It is going to take about four days.” Wei-Hao writes it in his notebook. Everything has a plan, and every plan has a date.

Monday 11:40 AM — The Decision

At 11:40 the lead developer pulls Wei-Hao aside. “The API team are changing the search format again. We will have to rewrite the part that reads the results.” Wei-Hao's first thought is that this will delay everything. But then he says, without deciding to: “I'll rewrite it today.” Nobody planned that sentence, and he does not think he will need the afternoon for it.

Tuesday 2:00 PM — The Prediction

By Tuesday afternoon Wei-Hao is sure the rewrite will not work with the old code. He reads the error log and says to his partner, “We're going to have problems on Friday if nobody changes the format. We are not going to finish by Wednesday at this rate.” His partner says, “Then we will change it today.” Neither of them knew that on Monday.

Wednesday 10:30 AM — The Promise

Wednesday is quieter. The manager asks whether the feature will be ready for the Friday demo. Wei-Hao had already decided on Monday to finish it early, so he answers: “It will be ready Thursday, not Friday — and the demo won't be affected.” And he is right.

Friday 4:00 PM — The Reflection

On Friday the demo runs smoothly and the manager thanks the team. Wei-Hao thinks back over the week. He made one plan on Monday: they are going to ship the search function, and the format rewrite is going to land the following week. He made one decision on Monday lunchtime. He made one prediction on Tuesday, from the error log. Three different future ideas — and three different English forms.

"A plan, a decision and a prediction are three different things. English uses three different forms for them, and choosing the right one is part of the message." — — Chen Wei-Hao, Junior Software Developer

Quick Review: The Two Future Forms

English has two ways to talk about the future. will is for decisions and promises; going to is for plans and predictions from evidence.

PatternExample
will + base verbI will rewrite the module tonight.
going to + base verbWe are going to add a search function.
will not / won'tIt won't be ready until Thursday.
going to (negative)We aren't going to change the design now.
Key Rules
  • For a plan made before now: We're going to add a search function this sprint.
  • For a decision made now: I'll rewrite it today. (Nobody said this an hour ago.)
  • For a promise: I will finish it by Friday.
  • For a prediction from present evidence: We're going to have problems on Friday.
  • Time expressions work with both: tomorrow, next week, in 2027, by Friday, in two hours
Common Mistakes (Chinese L1 Speakers)

Chinese speakers often join the two forms. ✗ I will going to rewrite it. → ✓ I will rewrite it. / ✓ I am going to rewrite it.

Also prefer going to for a plan you made earlier: ✗ I will go to the conference. (you decided last month) → ✓ I am going to go to the conference.

Activity A: Plan, Decision, or Prediction?

Put each sentence into the right column. Then say WHY you chose it.

  1. 1. We're going to release the update on Friday. going to — planned on Monday
  2. 2. I'll send you the file in a minute. will — decided just now
  3. 3. The tests are failing, so it's going to be a long night. going to — prediction from evidence
  4. 4. Don't worry, I will fix it before the demo. will — a promise
  5. 5. We aren't going to use that library any more. going to — an earlier plan, now cancelled
Example

“Sentence 3 is evidence-based: the speaker can see the failing tests, so the prediction is grounded in the present.”

Activity B: Your Own Sprint

Write three sentences about a project you are involved in: one plan, one spontaneous decision, and one prediction. Then swap with a partner and underline the form you used.

Try: “We're going to finish the login page by Thursday. I'll help you with the database part — I remember the query. If we don't test it tonight, we're going to find bugs at the weekend.”
