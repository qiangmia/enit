## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. Identify at least **8 common IT job titles** and describe their responsibilities.
2. Use **modals of ability** (*can, could, be able to*) to describe skills and qualifications.
3. Read and comprehend a short article about an IT professional's daily work.
4. Expand IT workplace vocabulary including technical terms, soft skills, and job-related actions.
5. Write a brief professional profile using target grammar and vocabulary.

# Day in the Life of a Database Administrator

*A look at a typical workday in Taiwan's tech industry*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name:** Lin Yichen (林奕辰) | **Age:** 26 |
| **Job:** Junior Database Administrator | **Company:** A mid-sized e-commerce company, Taipei |
| **Education:** B.A. in Information Management | |

---

#### 7:30 AM — Morning Routine

Yichen wakes up and checks his phone. He looks at the monitoring app. The app shows the status of the company's databases. Everything looks normal. No alerts. No emergencies. He drinks his coffee and gets ready for work.

#### 8:45 AM — Arrival at the Office

Yichen arrives at the office in the Neihu Technology Park. He turns on his computer and opens three screens. On the first screen, he checks the database performance dashboard. He can see how much data the system processed overnight. On the second screen, he reads his email. On the third screen, he reviews the task list for the day.

#### 9:00 AM — Morning Meeting

The team has a short meeting. Yichen's manager, Ms. Chen, asks everyone about their tasks. Yichen says, "I can finish the data migration project by Friday." His colleague, Wei, says he is working on a security update. Ms. Chen reminds them about the deadline for the new system. It must be ready before the company's biggest online sale event next month.

#### 9:30 AM — Database Work

Yichen starts his main task. He is migrating customer data from an old system to a new one. He writes SQL queries to extract the data. SQL is a language for talking to databases. He can read and write basic SQL very well. He is taking an online course to learn advanced SQL this month. He carefully checks that all the data is correct — customer names, addresses, and order history.

![Writing SQL queries](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1461749280684-dccba630e2f6?w=800&h=300&fit=crop)

*Writing SQL queries to manage database operations*

#### 11:00 AM — Problem Solving

Yichen finds a problem. Some customer records have duplicate entries. He needs to clean the data. He writes a script to remove the duplicates. He is able to fix most of the problems in 30 minutes. One difficult case takes an hour. He asks Wei for help. Together, they solve the problem.

#### 12:00 PM — Lunch Break

Yichen eats lunch with his team. They talk about work, but also about movies, games, and food. Yichen believes that good relationships with colleagues are important. "Technical skills are necessary," he says, "but you also need to communicate well with people."

#### 1:30 PM — Afternoon Tasks

After lunch, Yichen works on a backup system. He cannot allow data loss. He creates automatic backup scripts. The system will save copies of the database every six hours. If the main database fails, the company can restore the data from the backup.

#### 3:00 PM — Report and Communication

Yichen writes a weekly report for his manager. The report shows how the database performed this week. He uses a spreadsheet to create charts. He can use Excel well, but he is also learning Python for data analysis. Ms. Chen reads the report and asks questions about a spike in database usage on Tuesday.

#### 4:30 PM — Learning Time

The company gives employees one hour per week for learning. Yichen watches an online tutorial about cloud databases. He wants to learn Amazon RDS and Google Cloud SQL. He believes cloud technology will be important for his career. He takes notes.

#### 5:30 PM — End of Day

Yichen saves his work, closes his programs, and checks the monitoring app one more time. Everything is still normal. He says goodbye to his team and goes home. On the train, he reads a tech blog on his phone.

> "Start learning SQL early. It is one of the most useful skills for working with data. Also, practice solving problems — not just with computers, but with people too."
> — *Lin Yichen, Database Administrator*

### Category 2: Responsibilities & Actions

| Term | 中文 | Definition | Example Sentence |
|------|------|------------|------------------|
| **manage** | 管理 | To be in charge of something | She *can* manage a team of five people. |
| **maintain** | 維護 | To keep something in good condition | He *is able to* maintain servers running 24 hours a day. |
| **troubleshoot** | 故障排除 | To find and fix problems | They *can* troubleshoot hardware issues very quickly. |
| **deploy** | 部署 | To make a system or software ready to use | The team *can* deploy the new software by next week. |
| **migrate** | 遷移 | To move data or systems from one place to another | She *is able to* migrate data from the old system to the new one. |
| **monitor** | 監控 | To watch and check something over time | He *can* monitor the network for security problems. |
| **optimize** | 優化 | To make something work better | She *is able to* optimize the database to make it faster. |
| **analyze** | 分析 | To examine something in detail | He *can* analyze data to find business trends. |

### Vocabulary Activity: "Job + Action Chain" *(Speaking)*

**How to Play (10 min)**

1. Teacher calls out a **job title** (e.g., "DBA").
2. Students race to say a **responsibility** that matches, using *can*, *could*, or *is able to*.
3. First correct answer earns a point for that student's row/column.
4. Teacher then calls a **responsibility** — students must name the **job title** that fits.

**Examples**

- **Teacher:** "DBA!" → **Student:** "A DBA *can* migrate data."
- **Teacher:** "troubleshoot" → **Student:** "An IT support specialist *can* troubleshoot problems."

## Grammar Focus — Modals of Ability

### Review: Present Simple for Job Descriptions

We use the **present simple** to describe what people do in their jobs — their regular duties and responsibilities.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| I / You / We / They | base form | I **manage** databases. |
| He / She / It | base form + **-s** | She **manages** databases. |

**Common mistake (Chinese L1 speakers):** Don't forget the **-s** for he/she/it!

*✗ He **manage** databases.* → *✓ He **manages** databases.*

---

### Modals of Ability

| Modal | Meaning | When to Use | Example |
|-------|---------|-------------|---------|
| **can** | ability now | Something you are able to do now | She **can** write SQL queries. |
| **could** | past ability / possibility | Something you were able to do in the past, or a polite possibility | He **could** fix computers when he was 15. |
| **be able to** | formal ability | More formal than "can"; works in all tenses | She **is able to** manage complex databases. |

### Key Rules

- **Can** = present ability (informal). *"I can use Python."*
- **Could** = past ability OR polite possibility. *"I could swim when I was 10."* / *"Could you help me?"*
- **Be able to** = formal, and can be used in *any* tense (will be able to, have been able to, etc.).

**Contractions:** can't = cannot | couldn't = could not | is not able to = isn't able to
